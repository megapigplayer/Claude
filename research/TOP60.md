# TOP60 — the 60 best Apify Actor ideas, ranked and build-ready

Prepared 2026-09-28 from the four research files in this folder: `ideas_documents_files.md` (D01–D42), `ideas_validation_devtools.md` (V01–V49), `ideas_open_data.md` (OD-01–OD-45), `ideas_ai_transform.md` (T01–T49) — 185 ideas in total. No code is included; this is a planning document.

## 1. Method note and honest expected value

### 1.1 Method

- **Merge and de-duplicate.** 185 ideas were collapsed into 60 Actors. Main merges: D01 (Hebrew OCR) + D02 (RTL PDF text) into ONE Actor, because after verification OCR-with-Hebrew is a checkbox in 7+ Actors and only RTL-correct extraction is defensible; V11 + V12 + V13 into one Israeli validator; OD-23/24/25 (Hebcal) into four SEO-distinct Actors that share one library, plus two derived variants; T20 + T21 into one translator, with its deterministic half split out as a QA Actor; T33 + T34; T13 + T14; T41 + V45; OD-09 + OD-10; OD-17 + OD-18; T35 + T36 (text side); D05 + T36 (PDF side); D03 + D19 + D30 + T23; V07 + V05 + V09; T46 + V06 + D11 + D29. Everything the source files rejected stays rejected.
- **Verification pass (new, 2026-09-28).** All four files say the Store is saturated yet still list many "0 competitors" ideas. I re-checked every candidate that claimed 0–2 competitors with a targeted apify.com search (about 65 queries) and vetted every npm package named below (licence, last publish date) on the registry. Of 55 claims re-checked, **26 were contradicted** (an Actor already does it), **6 weakened**, **23 held** (Appendix A). One search either way is not proof: "held" means "not found", never "demand proven".
- **Blind spots.** docs.apify.com, api.apify.com and boi.org.il are egress-blocked from this environment (the CBS site was not tried; its details come from search snippets only). So PPE mechanics beyond the brief's "0.8 × revenue − compute" (start-event handling, minimum event prices) are assumed, and BOI/CBS API shapes come from search snippets. Both are flagged "verify".
- **Ranking** is a judgment ordering of: verified gap × a named buyer with recurring use × margin, discounted for build, legal and maintenance risk, ties broken toward offline-buildable. It is not a numeric score.
- **Tiers.** A = verified gap (0–1 close competitor, or an Israel- or regulation-specific niche where none exists) + a named buyer who can find it on Apify + recurring use. B = plausible (partial gap, thin or unclear demand, or gated). C = speculative or weak, included only to reach 60. **Complexity:** S ≤ 1 agent-day, M = 2–3, L = 4+.
- **"derived"** in the source-id column means a split, merge or variant I created from the listed ids; each still traces to them.
- **Conflicts between the source files.** V25 says German/French e-invoicing is uncovered; the D-file (with URLs) shows kamerozkan and josefbednar cover it, so the D-file wins and generic e-invoice validators are dropped (only Verifactu, Croatia and Belgium-specific extras remain). The T-file's "no input-only review analyzer" and the D-file's "no Hebrew OCR" were also wrong.

### 1.2 Expected value (judgment, not data)

**How saturation changes the odds.** A niche with 8 near-identical Actors splits its small search traffic 8 ways, and the repeat creators spotted in the OD-file (kamerozkan, junipr, benthepythondev, parseforge and others) can clone any niche that shows traction within days. The odds therefore come from niches where cloning is slow or pointless: Hebrew/Israel-specific rules (a language and regulation moat), format-fidelity work nobody has done (PO/ARB/XLIFF parsing, Meta feed repair, GraphQL diff), and calendar or checksum maths that LLM agents get wrong. But "no competitor" often means "no buyer": 26 of 55 zero/few-competitor claims were false, and the survivors mostly have small audiences and free upstream alternatives (Hebcal API, BOI site, CBS calculator, Spectral CLI). Price must therefore come from bulk, rules, monitoring and MCP/agent convenience, not from the data itself.

**Money math.** Net = 0.8 × price − compute. A tiny Node run costs roughly $0.0001–0.0005, so price dominates. $50/month net needs about $63 gross, which is about 3,100 events at $0.02, 6,300 at $0.01, 12,500 at $0.005, or about 125 runs at $0.50 (100–400 paid events a day). Daily tests on 60 Actors plus LLM canary calls add roughly $2–6/month of drag.

**Odds (all 60 live for 6 months).** P(total < $5/month) ≈ 30%; $5–25 ≈ 40%; $25–60 ≈ 22%; > $60 ≈ 8%. So P(reaching about $50) ≈ 15–20%, median ≈ $10–15, mean ≈ $20. About 70–75% of the expected revenue sits in about 20 ideas (**ranks 1–20 plus #23**, which shares #1's engine):
- the Jewish-calendar family (ranks 3, 4, 7, 14, 16): zero competitors, global diaspora plus agents, not just Israel;
- the Israeli finance/compliance set (5, 6, 8, 9, 12, 13);
- the global developer set (2, 10, 11);
- the e-commerce feed set (1, 20, 23);
- Hebrew documents and text (15, 17, 18, 19).

The remaining ideas (ranks 21–22 and 24–60) are cheap lottery tickets (mostly S/M, many offline). Build them after the ones above, and freeze any Actor with fewer than 5 paid events per month after 60 days. Realistic upside needs one or two Actors to get embedded in someone's recurring workflow (an n8n/Make flow or an agent tool); nothing here guarantees that.

## 4. Wave-1 per-Actor specs

### 4.0 Shared conventions (apply to all twelve)

- **Default inputs** must be tiny, deterministic and network-free: fixed historical dates, inline samples, bundled sample files; never "today". Every required field gets a `default` (a prefill alone is not enough for the daily test).
- **Charging.** Charge each event only after that unit of work succeeded (never for parse failures, empty rows or unsupported input); respect the run's maximum-total-charge limit and stop cleanly with a partial-result note; charge the start event once. Confirm current PPE rules on the Apify docs when network exists.
- **Data-driven rules.** Anything regulatory (thresholds, enums, holiday tables) lives in a versioned JSON file with `effectiveFrom`, `source` and `verifiedOn`; a tests-side freshness check fails loudly when `verifiedOn` is older than 90 days.
- **Determinism.** Sort outputs; no timestamps in dataset rows; pin dependency versions and Node major version.
- **Store SEO.** Title leads with the search phrase (under 60 characters); first README sentence reads as a tool description an agent can use ("Use this tool to …") because Apify Actors are exposed as MCP tools; include an example input and output, an FAQ and Hebrew keywords where relevant.
- **Libraries** named below were checked on the npm registry on 2026-09-28 (licence and last publish).

### 4.1 `israeli-id-validator` (rank 9, Tier A, S)

- **Input.** `values` (string array; default `123456782`, `123456789`, `IL620108000000099999999`, `050-1234567`, `2222222`); `type` (auto/id/company/iban/phone/postcode, default auto); `rejectDummy` (default true); `normalize` (default true).
- **Output per value.** `input`, `type`, `valid`, `reason` (bad-checksum, wrong-length, unknown-format, dummy, ambiguous), `normalized`, `details` (expected check digit, company-prefix hint, phone type and region, bank code and name), `warnings`.
- **Algorithm.** Zero-pad to 9 digits; weights 1,2 alternating; sum the digits of each product; valid if the total is divisible by 10 (same rule for ת.ז. and company numbers; the company-prefix class is informational only). Postcode = 7 digits.
- **Libraries.** `libphonenumber-js` (MIT; region IL: parse, validity, type); `ibantools` (MIT or MPL-2.0; Israel IBAN = 23 characters); static bank-code list (refresh from Bank of Israel when network exists).
- **Edge cases (input).** Leading zeros stripped by spreadsheets (8-digit IDs); dashes, spaces, RTL marks (U+200E/200F, U+202A–202E); Arabic-Indic and Persian digits; trunk zero in `+972 (0)50`; landline vs 07x VoIP vs 1-700/1-800.
- **Edge cases (semantics).** A 9-digit number can be valid as both person ID and company number, so flag `ambiguous`; a valid checksum never proves the person or company exists (say so in every result); all-zero and sequential dummies are rejected when `rejectDummy` is on.
- **Tests (golden).** `123456782` valid (expected check digit 2); `123456789` invalid; IBAN example `IL62 0108 0000 0009 9999 999` valid and invalid after changing the last digit; a `050-...` mobile normalizes to `+9725...`; an 8-digit ID is padded then checked.
- **Tests (limits).** RTL marks and Arabic-Indic digits stripped; 10,000-value batch under 3 seconds; default input finishes in seconds with no network.
- **PPE.** `value-checked` after each non-empty value is processed (invalid results are charged; blank or unparseable rows are not).
- **README/SEO.** "Israeli ID number validator (בדיקת תקינות תעודת זהות)", "Israeli company number ח.פ. checker", "IL IBAN validator", bulk CSV, n8n/Make. Say plainly: checksum only, no registry lookup, nothing stored.
- **Notes.** Builds the shared Israeli-checks package reused by #6 and #13, so it ships first.

### 4.2 `hebrew-date-converter` (rank 7, Tier A, S)

- **Input.** `dates` (ISO dates or Hebrew-date objects; default `2026-09-12`, `2026-09-28`); `direction` (auto/g2h/h2g); `afterSunset` (default false; when true the Hebrew date rolls forward a day); `language` (en/he/both, default both); `mode` (convert or anniversary, default convert) with `anniversaryYears` (default 5).
- **Output.** `gregorian` (ISO), `weekday`, Hebrew day, month (name and number) and year, `hebrewString`, `hebrewStringHe` (gematria, e.g. י״ז בתשרי תשפ״ז), `isLeapYear`, optional `holiday` and `parasha`; anniversary mode returns the next N Gregorian dates plus a rule note.
- **Algorithm.** Convert with the library; own gematria formatter (15 and 16 written ט״ו and ט״ז); explicit, reported rules for 30 Cheshvan, 30 Kislev and Adar I/II in anniversaries.
- **Libraries.** `jewish-date` (MIT), or `@hebcal/hdate` (GPL-2.0, see Appendix B); Node's built-in Intl Hebrew calendar is used only as a test oracle.
- **Edge cases.** Sunset boundary; invalid dates (30 Kislev in a short year); "Adar" in a leap year is ambiguous (reject or ask); DD/MM vs MM/DD ambiguity (accept ISO or an explicit format only); proleptic Gregorian before 1582.
- **Tests (anchors).** 2026-09-12 = 1 Tishrei 5787; 2026-09-21 = 10 Tishrei (Yom Kippur); 2026-09-28 = 17 Tishrei 5787; 2026-05-22 = 6 Sivan 5786 (Shavuot); 5787 is a leap year.
- **Tests (oracle).** Compare every date from 1900 to 2100 with Node's Intl Hebrew calendar; g2h then h2g round trip; gematria strings for 5786 and 5787 (תשפ״ו, תשפ״ז).
- **PPE.** `date-converted` per successfully converted date.
- **README/SEO.** "Hebrew date converter API", "Gregorian to Hebrew date", "תאריך עברי", "yahrzeit", bulk conversion for Google Sheets, sunset handling.

### 4.3 `jewish-holidays-calendar` (rank 3, Tier A, S)

- **Input.** `year` (default 2026); `yearType` (gregorian/hebrew); `location` (israel/diaspora, default israel); `include` (major, minor, fasts, roshChodesh, modern, omer, parasha, specialShabbat; default major and fasts); `language` (en/he/both); `format` (json/csv/ics, default json).
- **Output rows.** `date`, `hebrewDate` (+`He`), `name` (+`He`), `category`, `beginsEveningBefore`, `isYomTov`, `isIsraeliPublicHoliday` (israel only), `memo`; plus a meta row (leap year, year length).
- **Algorithm.** Generate events for the year with the Israel/diaspora flag and Hebrew locale; flag the nine statutory Israeli holiday days from an explicit table (Rosh Hashanah days 1–2, Yom Kippur, Sukkot day 1, Shemini Atzeret/Simchat Torah, Pesach days 1 and 7, Yom HaAtzmaut, Shavuot).
- **Libraries.** `@hebcal/core` (GPL-2.0, hosted use; Appendix B). Fallback if the licence is rejected: `jewish-date` (MIT) plus an own holiday-rules table.
