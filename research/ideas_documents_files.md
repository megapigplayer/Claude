# Document & File Processing — Apify Actor Market Research

Research date: 2026-09-28. Category: PDF, Office/data-format conversion, OCR, image processing, QR/barcode, subtitles, archives, EXIF, HTML→PDF, and EU e-invoicing.

## Key findings (read this first)

1. **Every "obvious" niche in this category is already heavily saturated on Apify Store.** PDF merge/split/compress (3+ near-identical all-in-ones), PDF text/table extraction to JSON/CSV (8+ competitors), bulk image WebP/AVIF conversion (6+ near-identical), QR code generation (6+), universal JSON/YAML/XML/CSV converters (5+), Pandoc-based Markdown converters (7+), CSV/JSON schema validators (5+), and EXIF strippers (3+, plus bundled into most image actors) all have multiple close clones already live. [evidence: apify.com search results captured 2026-09-28, see table]
2. **HTML→PDF/screenshot is the single most saturated sub-niche found** (10+ actors: apify/html-to-pdf-converter, moonweil, lizaraco, al_mansouri, sornkan, vojtam, ohsiingh, clearrun, automation-lab, louisdeconinck, exciting_perfume) and it structurally requires a headless browser (heavy compute) — this is a clear avoid, doubly disqualified by both saturation and compute cost. [evidence: apify.com/apify/html-to-pdf-converter and others]
3. **EU e-invoicing — the niche the brief flagged as most promising — is already being aggressively and systematically mined**, mainly by one prolific seller (`kamerozkan`, with `josefbednar` covering ZUGFeRD/Factur-X) who has published ~9 actors covering Peppol BIS, XRechnung, ZUGFeRD/Factur-X, FatturaPA, France, and Poland KSeF FA(3) — both validators and generators, several with pinned/offline schema validation. [evidence: apify.com/kamerozkan/* URLs found in search, listed in table] This is the single most important finding for this category: the "easy" e-invoicing plays are taken. The remaining opportunity is in the corners that lineup does not yet cover (Belgium, Spain, Croatia, cross-format conversion, Israel) or in genuinely different sub-niches (Hebrew/RTL, subtitle QC, diffing, accessibility).
4. **The regulatory calendar is real and confirmed**, which validates recurring B2B demand even though supply has caught up: Poland KSeF mandatory for large filers 1 Feb 2026, most businesses 1 Apr 2026, micro-entrepreneurs 1 Jan 2027, no penalties before 2028 [evidence: vatcalc.com, dudkowiak.com, vatupdate.com]; France live for large/mid enterprises issuing since 1 Sept 2026, SMEs follow 2027 [evidence: fiskaly.com]; Germany mandatory receiving since 2025, issuing Jan 2027 (>€800k turnover) / Jan 2028 (rest) [evidence: edicomgroup.com, learninggov.com]; Italy mandatory since 2019 with EU derogation extended to Dec 2027 [evidence: zoneandco.com, dddinvoices.com]; EU-wide ViDA Digital Reporting Requirements mandatory 1 July 2030, member states free to go earlier [evidence: edicomgroup.com, gerlach-customs.com].
5. **Hebrew/RTL document processing is a genuine white space** — no competing Apify actor surfaced in any search this session (PDF, OCR, or e-invoicing searches), Tesseract Hebrew accuracy is usable (92–96% on clean modern print) [evidence: mf-sr.com], and this matches the owner's stated language/country edge. This is the strongest differentiated opportunity found.
6. **Pricing signal**: mid-range PPE pricing outperforms rock-bottom — a $0.10/result actor with 5,000 monthly results ($500) beats a $0.02/result actor needing 25,000 results to match [evidence: apifyforge.com/blog/how-to-price-your-apify-actor]. One observed subtitle-conversion actor prices at $0.005/file with no start fee — a race-to-the-bottom price point in that saturated niche [evidence: apify.com/eliai/srt-to-vtt-converter]. E-invoicing/compliance tools can justify materially higher per-event prices given B2B stakes (external Peppol access points charge ~$0.10/invoice pay-as-you-go up to enterprise contracts) [evidence: valtatech.com/thought-leadership/einvoicing/how-much-does-a-peppol-einvoice-cost].
7. **Evidence gaps, flagged honestly**: Belgium/Spain/Croatia/Israel mandate specifics below are [judgment] (general knowledge, not verified via WebSearch this session — the 25-query budget was spent on higher-priority confirmations). A dedicated `site:apify.com Hebrew OCR` query was not run either; its "no competition found" status rests on absence of hits across five adjacent OCR/PDF-extraction searches, not a direct check — worth a cheap direct verification before building.

Search budget used: 25/25 WebSearch calls (shared budget). WebFetch not used (per method note, tried at most once was not needed — search snippets were sufficient).

## 2. Top 20 ideas — justification

**D01 — Hebrew OCR for scans (score 9).** No competing Apify actor surfaced across five adjacent OCR/PDF-extraction searches this session, and Tesseract's Hebrew accuracy (92–96% on clean modern print [ev: mf-sr.com/en/blog/ocr-hebrew-2026-practitioner-guide.html]) is good enough for a genuinely useful paid tool. It directly matches the owner's stated language/country edge, needs no headless browser, runs entirely offline once the Hebrew traineddata is bundled, and serves a real recurring workflow (Israeli law firms, archives, and genealogy researchers digitizing paper records). Default input can be one small bundled sample scan, keeping the daily test well under two minutes.

**D02 — RTL-aware PDF extractor (score 8).** Generic PDF-to-text/table extractors (8+ found on Apify) are built and tested against LTR Latin text; Hebrew/Arabic bidi reordering is a well-known failure mode they don't specifically solve. Building an extractor that guarantees correct logical reading order for RTL scripts is a real technical differentiator, not just a relabeled clone, and shares the same low-compute, offline-testable profile as D01. Pairs naturally with D01 as a two-actor RTL suite.

**D03 — Subtitle QC/validator (score 7).** Every subtitle actor found this session converts or generates; none validates. Streaming/broadcast localization vendors have hard delivery specs (max characters-per-second, no overlapping cues, valid timecodes) that a converter doesn't enforce. The existing $0.005/file pricing on converters [ev: apify.com/eliai/srt-to-vtt-converter] proves willingness to pay in this space even at thin margins; a QC gate that prevents costly re-deliveries can be priced higher. Purely offline, sub-second per file.

**D04 — PDF diff / change monitor (score 7).** Only one direct competitor found (abiding_knapweed), which both proves demand exists and leaves room for a second entrant with a cleaner UX or narrower vertical focus (e.g., contract redlines for legal teams specifically). It is one of the few ideas in this category that naturally fits Apify's scheduled-run model — exactly the "recurring/scheduled" bonus criterion the brief calls out. Default input should bundle two small sample PDFs rather than fetch live URLs, to keep the daily test offline and fast.

**D05 — PDF PII redactor (score 6).** Two live competitors (northbound_works, craigtechservicesllc) confirm GDPR-driven demand, but their descriptions show room for differentiation (verified-unextractable redaction, batch mode, structured audit log). Legal basis is clean since only user-supplied PDFs are processed. Compute is light (PyMuPDF-style redaction, no OCR needed for text-layer PDFs).

**D06 — Excel/CSV diff & reconciliation (score 7).** No dedicated diff/reconciliation tool turned up despite multiple CSV/JSON tooling searches — the existing actors clean, validate, and convert, but none compares two versions of a spreadsheet. Month-end reconciliation is a textbook recurring B2B accounting workflow, entirely offline, and cheap to build on existing CSV-parsing libraries.

**D07 — Belgium Peppol B2B validator (score 7).** Belgium's Peppol B2B mandate takes effect 1 Jan 2026 [judgment — general regulatory knowledge, not re-verified via search this session], and the incumbent e-invoicing seller's known lineup (Peppol BIS generic, XRechnung, ZUGFeRD, FatturaPA, France, Poland KSeF) does not mention Belgium specifically. No PDF/UA or accessibility-tagging checker surfaced anywhere in this research, despite extensive PDF-tooling searches, suggesting real white space. Parsing PDF tag structure is light, offline work (pikepdf/pdf.js-style), and the buyer (compliance/legal teams doing recurring audits) is a strong B2B fit.

**D08 — Spain Veri*Factu / Crea y Crece validator (score 6).** Spain's B2B e-invoicing and anti-fraud (Veri*Factu) rules are rolling out through 2026–2027 [judgment, unverified this session], and no Apify actor for Spain specifically surfaced in any e-invoicing search. **D09 — Cross-format e-invoice converter (score 6).** The incumbent already proved this exact pattern works by shipping a ZUGFeRD→XRechnung converter [ev: apify.com/kamerozkan/zugferd-to-xrechnung-converter], but has not (per the actors surfaced) built out the full matrix (e.g., FatturaPA↔Peppol UBL, or anything↔KSeF FA(3)). **D11 — GS1 barcode compliance validator/generator (score 6).** Generic barcode generators are already live and multiple, but none frames itself around GS1 check-digit/prefix compliance for marketplace listing requirements — a concrete pain point for e-commerce sellers who get listings rejected for invalid GTINs.
