# Validation, Data-Quality, Developer & Website-Audit Actor Ideas
Research date: 2026-09-28. Category scope per brief: checksum/format validators (IBAN/BIC/VAT/EORI/LEI/tax-ID/company-ID), phone/address normalization, JSON Schema/OpenAPI/GraphQL validation, SBOM/license/OSV supply-chain checks, DNS/SSL/security-header/email-auth audits, broken-link/sitemap/schema.org/tech-stack website audits, uptime/SSL/WHOIS monitoring, cron/regex/timezone utilities, CSV data cleaning.

## Key finding before the tables

This category is **dramatically more saturated on Apify than the brief's framing suggests**. A `site:apify.com`-style search was run for essentially every sub-niche named in the brief, and in nearly every case **5–10+ competing Actors already exist**, often with near-identical titles ("Bulk SSL Checker", "SSL Certificate Expiry Checker", "TLS Certificate Expiry Checker", "Domain & SSL Expiry Monitor" — four different builders, same product). The pattern held for IBAN/BIC (6+), EU VAT/VIES (9+), LEI/GLEIF (6+), phone validation (8+), SSL/DNS/security-header audits (9+), SPF/DKIM/DMARC (9+), broken-link/sitemap checking (7+), tech-stack detection (8+), uptime monitoring (9+), WHOIS/RDAP (8+), cron utilities (6+), CSV cleaning (8+), typosquat/brand monitoring (6+), OpenAPI breaking-change diff (5+), and SBOM/OSV vulnerability scanning (3+ dedicated, plus a bundled 5-Actor security catalog covering Trivy/Gitleaks/Grype/OSV-Scanner). **Importantly, the sub-niche the brief calls "best" — scheduled/recurring monitoring (uptime, SSL/domain expiry, WHOIS, typosquat) — is precisely the most saturated part of the whole category**, because it is the most obvious recurring-revenue idea and many builders have already had it.

Two consequences for this report: (1) the "top 20" below leans toward genuine gaps and thin-competition niches found during research rather than the brief's headline ideas, most of which score low here specifically because they are saturated; (2) a wider net was cast within the stated category (national ID checksum validators beyond the EU, e-invoicing formats, MCP-server validation, a few plausible-but-unverified website-audit gaps) to reach the required 35–50 ideas while being honest about which are evidence-backed vs. judgment calls. Search-snippet results did not surface exact user counts or star ratings for Apify listings, so "competition" below is a **verified count of distinct competing Actors** rather than their popularity; that is a real limitation of this research pass.

Budget note: 25 WebSearch calls were used (shared pool), batched 3–5 keywords per query. No WebFetch/curl was used (not needed; Apify listing pages returned rich snippets).

## Part 2 — Top 20, with justification

**1. V20 — GraphQL breaking-change CI diff checker (score 7).** OpenAPI has 5 competing breaking-change-diff Actors; GraphQL has exactly 1 (junipr) despite serving the same CI/DevOps buying motive and being just as real a workflow — teams running GraphQL APIs still need to gate PRs on schema compatibility. Pure spec-diff logic, zero network dependency, fully offline-testable, and trivially cheap to run. The main risk is a small absolute market (GraphQL is less common than REST), but the competitive gap is the clearest ratio (5:1 → 1:0) found in the entire category.

**2. V25 — EU e-invoicing format validator, expanding beyond Italy/Poland (score 7).** One builder (kamerozkan) has already proven the model works for Italy's FatturaPA and Poland's KSeF, but Germany's XRechnung/ZUGFeRD and France's Factur-X — both backed by hard regulatory deadlines in the EU's e-invoicing rollout — were not found on Apify. Pure XSD validation against officially published schemas is low-compute and offline-testable. Maintenance risk is real (schemas evolve with the mandates) but that same regulatory pressure is exactly what creates urgent, recurring demand from ERP/accounting vendors.

**3. V10 — Passport MRZ (ICAO 9303) checksum + field validator (score 7).** Zero competitors found anywhere in this search pass, a stable 20+-year-old public standard, and a real buyer in travel-tech/KYC-document-pipeline QA. It is pure string-parsing (no network ever), so it is both the cheapest possible Actor to run and the easiest to keep passing Apify's daily default-input test. The one required caveat: it processes personal identity-document numbers even in single-record test mode, so the Actor description should be explicit about being stateless/non-storing.

**4. V42 — Regex tester/explainer/benchmarker (score 7).** The same builder who has 6 competitors in cron tooling has only 1 competitor (themselves) in regex tooling, despite regex being at least as universal a developer need. Pure utility, offline-testable, trivial compute, and a natural fit as an MCP tool for coding agents that need to validate a pattern before using it.

**5. V9 — ABN (Australia)/NZBN (NZ) business-number validator (score 6).** No Apify competitor found for either identifier, unlike the deeply saturated EU VAT/IBAN space. AU/NZ GST-registered businesses need ABN validity checks constantly, and the checksum-only mode is fully offline; the free-key live-lookup mode should be clearly flagged per the legal criteria. Realistic risk: the market is smaller than the EU, and demand is inferred rather than directly evidenced.

**6. V11 — Israeli ID (Teudat Zehut) checksum bulk validator (score 6).** Directly matches the brief's Israeli-owner bonus edge and is a confirmed zero-competitor gap after a dedicated search. Pure offline public algorithm, trivially cheap, no maintenance surface. Scored a 6 rather than higher because personal ID checksum-checking is more often a one-off dev utility than a scheduled B2B workflow, so the realistic revenue ceiling is modest — but modest is exactly what a $50/month-across-60-Actors portfolio needs.

**7. V12 — Israeli company number (ח״פ/ע״מ) checksum-only validator (score 6).** Four Actors already do full Israeli company-registry lookups (bovi, parseforge, foxlabs, scrapers_lat), proving real demand for Israeli company data — but none appear to offer a lightweight, offline, checksum-only check as a distinct fast-path product. Cheap to build and run, and can legitimately be marketed as "instant, no registry call needed" against the four existing (slower, registry-dependent) competitors.

**8. V15 — Postal address normalizer, libpostal-based (score 6).** Only one competitor found (rl1987) versus 8 for phone-number validation, despite address hygiene being at least as constant a data-quality pain for logistics/e-commerce teams — failed deliveries and address-format mismatches are a recurring, not one-off, cost center. Fully offline once the libpostal model is bundled, so it stays cheap and fast despite being a heavier library than a pure checksum.

**9. V18 — GraphQL SDL schema validator/linter (score 6).** Distinct job from the breaking-change checker (V20): this validates a single schema's correctness/style rather than diffing two versions. Only generic schema-conversion tools were found nearby, no dedicated GraphQL linter. Offline, cheap, and pairs naturally with V20 as a two-Actor GraphQL-tooling mini-suite.

**10. V24 — Open-source license compliance/conflict scanner (score 6).** The CVE/vulnerability side of supply-chain scanning is thoroughly saturated (3+ dedicated Actors plus a 5-Actor bundled catalog), but the license-conflict side was mentioned only once, in passing, as a single competitor's product. Legal risk from license conflicts is a genuine, recurring CI-gate concern for any company shipping software with third-party dependencies, and it's a natural complement to sell alongside (not instead of) the crowded CVE-scanner space.

**11. V30 — security.txt / .well-known files validator (score 6).** No competitor found, distinct from the well-covered SSL-cert/security-header/DNS cluster (9+ Actors) because those check TLS and headers, not RFC 9116 security.txt or mobile deep-link association files. Light compute, fetches only the user's own domain. Scored a 6 rather than higher because adoption of security.txt itself is still a niche practice, capping realistic volume.

**12. V32 — CORS configuration tester (score 6).** No dedicated Actor found despite CORS misconfiguration being one of the most common real-world API debugging complaints developers have. Testing the user's own endpoint's preflight/actual-request behavior is legally clean, cheap, and a good fit for both human developers and coding agents validating an API before shipping it (MCP-tool bonus).

**13. V36 — hreflang/multi-language SEO tag validator (score 6).** Distinct from the 3 existing schema.org/structured-data validators; no dedicated hreflang checker was found despite hreflang misconfiguration being a textbook, recurring SEO-agency audit item for any multilingual site. Moderate compute (crawls the user's declared alternate pages) but can be capped to a tiny default input.

**14. V43 — Timezone-safe date & business-day calculator (score 6).** Cron tooling is saturated (6+ competitors) but a standalone date-arithmetic/business-day utility was not found as its own product — only as a feature bolted onto cron-next-run calculators. Pure IANA-tzdata utility, zero network, and a strong candidate as a small, composable MCP tool that scheduling-oriented agents would call repeatedly.

**15. V46 — MCP server schema/security validator (score 6).** Only 2 competitors found in a category that is visibly growing fast (Apify's own MCP integration push, a dedicated "Best MCP Server Actors" content page). Real, current demand from AI-agent developers vetting servers before connecting them. Scored a 6 rather than higher specifically because the MCP ecosystem is moving quickly enough that today's 2-competitor gap could close fast, and because it only stays legally clean when scoped to servers the user owns or has permission to test.

**16. V05 — ISIN standalone checksum validator (score 5).** Bundled inside exactly one multi-validator Actor (mangudai) but not offered as its own focused product; every fund administrator/security-master data team validates ISINs. Pure offline mod-10 checksum, trivial to build and run, but the addressable market is narrower and more specialist than IBAN/VAT.

**17. V07 — Multi-country tax/company-ID checksum bundle (score 5).** Individually, most countries in scope already have 0–1 dedicated Actor (Spain, Poland, Brazil, China each have exactly one), so a single Actor covering 10+ countries' checksums in one call is a real breadth differentiator for marketplaces/AP teams onboarding international sellers — but it's unverified whether buyers prefer one broad tool over per-country specialists, and it directly overlaps with several existing single-country Actors.

**18. V17 — OpenAPI spec linter, style/rule-based (score 5).** Distinct job from the 5 existing OpenAPI breaking-change-diff Actors (those compare two versions; this lints one spec against style/completeness rules, the Spectral use case). No dedicated Actor found for this specific job, though the adjacent breaking-change niche is crowded enough to suggest the OpenAPI-tooling space overall gets picked over quickly.

**19. V31 — CSP header generator, proactive (score 5).** Every competitor found in the CSP space is a checker/grader of an existing policy; a generator that inventories a site's actual scripts/resources and proposes a policy is a different, complementary job. Speculative demand, and it requires fetching the user's live site, but stays light-compute and legally clean (user's own domain).

**20. V45 — Fuzzy-match record deduplication (score 5).** The 8 existing CSV-cleaner Actors all appear to do exact-match or simple-normalize dedup; a true fuzzy-matching engine (Jaro-Winkler/phonetic, catching "Jon Smith" vs. "John Smyth" or minor address variants) is a real, differentiated data-quality job that plain trim-and-dedupe tools don't do. CPU-bound but boundable to a tiny default input; risk is that it's unverified whether any of the 8 incumbents already quietly does this under a generic "clean & normalize" description.

## Methodology notes / limitations

- Competition counts are from Google-indexed Apify Store listing pages found via WebSearch; they undercount if a competitor exists but wasn't indexed or phrased differently, and search snippets did not reliably surface exact user/run counts or star ratings, so "competition" here measures **distinct competing Actors found**, not their relative popularity or revenue.
- 25 WebSearch calls were used this session (shared budget); no WebFetch/curl calls were needed or attempted.
- [judgment]-tagged rows have no direct search evidence of Apify-side competition or demand and should be treated as hypotheses to validate further (e.g., via Apify's own Ideas board or a direct Store search) before committing build time, not as confirmed gaps.
- Given how saturated almost every explicitly-named idea in the brief turned out to be, the operator should weight this report's top picks (thin-competition/zero-competition gaps and differentiated angles on saturated themes) over the brief's own category description when deciding what to actually build.
